# Keeper log: the Afanasyev calibration

**2026-10-04. The protocol frozen first** (`CalibrationProtocol.md`, second project ef766e7), before the transcriber repository held rules or prompts.

**The texts.** 45 tales, A093 to A167, the Appendix III tales, from Russian Wikisource (the 1984-85 edition), cut out by number with `extract.py` in Propp-Afanasyev (2f0dc14). A126 is Belarusian, A143 strongly dialectal.

**The rules and prompts, committed before any run** (Propp-Afanasyev, see its log). The rules are the Aesop rules, chosen because the tales are narratives, as the fables are, and the fables carry the strongest block findings; the definitions card of arm U is the Aesop card unchanged, checked byte for byte; arm C adds one sentence to θ. The other changes are "fable" to "tale", a placeholder example identifier (A000), the moral rule softened since few tales have one, and a paragraph on reading in the original language and writing in English. Three batches of 15 tales: b1 A093 to A127, b2 A128 to A148, b3 A149 to A167. Nothing in the repository says Propp analyzed these tales.

**The events stage, from master at de8fd5c: in form.** 45 tales, 1,633 events (460, 589 and 584 by batch), numbered from 1 in each tale, one hero line each, no ties; joined unchanged into `Events.txt` and `Heroes.txt` (Propp-Afanasyev). The three listers worked in one folder at once and saw each other's working-file names; they worked on different tales, and no list shows another's.

**The comparison program, committed before any transcription or recalled scheme was read** (`Calibration/compare.py`). It implements the protocol and fixes the readings the protocol leaves open: ours is the primary reading (marked Y, doubtful included) in the order of the notes; Propp's is `embedCorpus.derive()` as it stands, which carries the shared opener into a brace row in 163 I and 164 II; our move count is the distinct move labels the transcriber wrote; acceptance builds moves as the tragedy test does. Tested on a synthetic repository built from Propp's own schemes (a copy of them scores 1.000; the baseline, probe and verdicts behave), and v46 accepts 80 of Propp's 84 moves, as the protocol says.

**RESULT, 2026-10-05** (`compare.py`, output `CompareRun.txt`, run on Propp-Afanasyev ae97d30): **PARTLY CALIBRATED, in both arms and for both transcribers.**
- The baseline criterion holds everywhere: content 0.58 to 0.59 against Propp, the derangements 0.40 to 0.42; order 0.34 to 0.37 against 0.20 to 0.21; 0 of 1000 derangements at or above in every cell. The transcriptions are about the tale in front of them, not about wondertales in general.
- Content falls just short of the yardstick: 0.77 to 0.79 of the A-B agreement (0.75), against 0.80 needed.
- Order falls well short: 0.58 to 0.63 of the A-B agreement (0.59).
- Acceptance fails badly: v46 accepts 22.5% to 28.9% of our moves (129 to 152 of them, against Propp's 84), where it accepts 80 of Propp's 84. Our move counts equal Propp's in 29% to 38% of tales.
- Arm C against arm U: no change beyond noise on any measure; the θ sentence does not move the calibration.
- Contact check: each transcriber's C against its own U, content 0.82 and 0.87, order 0.67 and 0.77, against A-B 0.75 and 0.59. Higher, as the same model reading the same event lists under nearly the same rules should be; not so far above that contact is indicated, and not so close to 1 that it is ruled out.
- The memory probe: MEMORY IMPLAUSIBLE. Opus recalled one scheme, Sonnet none; recall content is 0.57 and 0.59 below the transcriptions'.
- The four hero-rule tales (149, 151, 163, 164) are not outliers.
NOT IN THE PROTOCOL, COMPUTED AFTER THE RESULT AND REPORTED ONLY: coverage, events with a function entry kept by the primary reading, is 62.3% to 67.1% (secondary 62.7% to 71.8%), against the 80% the tragedy and Apollodorus tests required and the 60% of the Aesop test, where Aesop scored 56% to 57%.
WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE EARLIER TESTS, the keeper's reading: the genre tests' thresholds for acceptance (85.2%) and coverage (80%) were set from Propp's own schemes, and the instrument does not reach them on Propp's own tales. On acceptance it scores Propp's tales (22% to 29%) below Aesop's fables (57% to 59%). So the acceptance and coverage failures of Apollodorus, Aesop and the tragedies measure the instrument at least as much as the genre, and cannot by themselves show that those genres differ from the wondertale. The block findings are untouched: they compare the instrument's output with shuffles of itself, not with Propp's levels. Genre comparisons should now be instrument to instrument, with these 45 transcribed tales as the Russian reference.

**DIAGNOSTICS AFTER THE RESULT, 2026-10-05, not frozen, reported only** (arm U; the owner asked how bad the content figure is):
- Beyond chance: content agreement corrected for the derangement baseline, (real - chance) / (1 - chance), is 0.57 between A and B and 0.30 to 0.31 between either and Propp; order 0.49 against 0.19 to 0.20. The frozen raw ratio (0.78) flatters, because chance content is high (0.41).
- Over-marking: the transcribers write 1,058 and 1,157 function symbols where Propp prints 737. They find 74% and 77% of Propp's symbols, and only 51% and 49% of theirs are in his schemes. F, I, a, M and N appear well over twice as often as in Propp; C (0.68), T (0.67), down (0.84) and Pr (0.85) less often.
- As sets, each function counted once per tale: content 0.75 and 0.78, 80% and 84% of Propp's functions found, 75% of ours in his. So the instrument mostly agrees with Propp on WHICH functions a tale holds; it differs on how often they recur and, most, on order (0.40 and 0.41 even as sets). The length difference alone caps the order measure at 0.64 to 0.66.

**THE IMPROVEMENT TEST (`ImproveProtocol.md`, frozen at 6fb2600 with `Split.txt`): development, 2026-10-05, on the 22 development tales only** (`devHarness.py`, then `improveTest.py dev`, output `ImproveDev.txt`).
- Order diagnosed: tales where Propp prints braces or embedded moves agree on order slightly BETTER than plain ones (0.36 and 0.37 against 0.33 and 0.34), so the flattening of braces does not explain the order gap.
- Tried, each on all four readings: note order against event order (no difference); collapsing immediately repeated runs of one to four functions (content about +0.07, order about +0.07); an implicit C before a departure that follows A, a or B with no C (about +0.01 each); each function once per move, first or last occurrence; each function once per tale; consensus of the four readings at 1, 2, 3 and 4 votes, alone and with the other rules.
- CONSENSUS DID NOT HELP: at its best (3 of 4 votes, with the other rules) it equals a single reading, so the four readings' departures from Propp are largely shared, not independent noise.
- CHOSEN, by the best mean of content and order over the four readings: the PROPP VIEW, each function once per move where it first appears, then the implicit C. Development gains: content +0.09 to +0.13, order +0.09 to +0.11, all p at most 0.0003; chance-corrected content from 0.20-0.29 to 0.29-0.40. v46 acceptance rises only from about 24% to 27-35%: the move division remains the transcribers' (62 to 76 moves on these tales). Ten or so variants were tried, which is why the test half must show at least half the development gain.
- The decision rule's one open point fixed before the test: ADOPTED needs all three criteria in ALL FOUR readings.

**THE IMPROVEMENT TEST, RESULT, 2026-10-05** (`improveTest.py test`, run once, output `ImproveTestRun.txt`): **NOT ADOPTED.** On the 23 test tales the Propp view raises content in all four readings, +0.040 to +0.058, each p at most 0.0036, but only one reading (C/B) reaches half its development gain; order rises +0.018 to +0.030, significant in one reading (U/A, p 0.037), not in the others. Every reading still beats the derangements (0 of 1000). v46 acceptance stays 21% to 34%. The instrument as written stands.
WHAT IT MEANS, the keeper's reading: folding repetitions explains a small, real part of the content gap (about 0.05 of the 0.4 between the instrument and full agreement) and almost none of the order gap. The development gain was about twice the test gain, the usual sign of a choice fitted to the tales it was chosen on, which the half-gain criterion was there to catch.
A STATEMENT OF MINE WITHDRAWN: I told the owner that "most of the gap is bookkeeping, not misreading", resting on the once-per-tale diagnostic. That diagnostic folded Propp's schemes as well as ours, so it measured agreement on a coarser question, not a correction of the instrument. Folding ours alone, tested on unseen tales, closes only a small part of the gap. Most of the disagreement is substantive: the transcribers mark functions where Propp marks none (F, a and I above all), and divide moves differently.
