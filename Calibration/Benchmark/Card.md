# The definitions card

The card the model transcribers used (arm U), unchanged.


**These definitions, in plain words, stand in for Propp's own chapters.**
They follow his definitions closely, but his text is not reproduced.
Read "hero" and "villain" as the roles an act gives, not as fixed characters: the rule above applies whoever performs the act.

| Symbol | Function | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| α | initial situation | The characters and their situation are introduced. Not a function; write it only for an event that does nothing but introduce. An event that narrates an act is never α. |
| β | absentation | A member of a household, or the one who protects it, goes away or dies, leaving others unprotected. |
| γ | interdiction | Someone is forbidden to do something, or ordered to do something. |
| δ | violation | The prohibition is broken, or the order is not obeyed. |
| ε | reconnaissance | The villain tries to find out something about the victim: where they are, or what they have. |
| ζ | delivery | The villain receives that information. |
| η | trickery | The villain, often in disguise or with false words, tries to deceive the victim in order to get hold of them or what they have. |
| θ | complicity | The victim is taken in by the deception and so unwittingly helps the villain. |
| λ | preliminary misfortune | A misfortune that forces the victim to accept the villain's deceitful bargain. |
| A | villainy | The villain does harm: kills, carries off, steals, injures, drives out, deceives to ruin, or otherwise causes loss or misfortune. |
| a | lack | Someone lacks or desires something: food, safety, a mate, a possession, a quality they do not have. |
| B | mediation | The misfortune or lack is made known to someone who might act on it; the hero is asked, ordered, allowed, or sent to act. A lament or complaint to no one who might act is X. |
| C | beginning counteraction | The hero decides or agrees to act against the misfortune or lack. |
| ↑ | departure | The hero leaves home or sets out. |
| D | first function of the donor | The hero is tested, questioned, asked for help, or attacked by someone who may then give help. |
| E | the hero's reaction | The hero responds to that test or request: passes or fails it, helps or refuses, withstands or yields. |
| F | receipt of an agent | The hero gets something or someone that will help: given, earned, bought, found, seized, or a helper offering service. |
| G | guidance | The hero is led, carried, or shown the way to what is sought. |
| H | struggle | The hero and the villain meet in direct combat or contest. |
| J | branding | The hero is marked: wounded, or given a token that can later identify them. |
| I | victory | The villain is defeated: killed, beaten, outwitted, or driven off. |
| K | liquidation | The original misfortune or lack is set right: the loss restored, the want satisfied, the victim freed. |
| ↓ | return | The hero returns to where they set out from. |
| Pr | pursuit | The hero is pursued. |
| Rs | rescue | The hero is saved from pursuit or from destruction. |
| o | unrecognized arrival | The hero arrives, at home or elsewhere, without being recognized. |
| L | unfounded claims | A false hero claims what is not theirs: a deed, a reward, a quality. |
| M | difficult task | A difficult task or test is set. |
| N | solution | The task is accomplished. |
| Q | recognition | The hero is recognized. |
| Ex | exposure | The false hero or the villain is exposed. |
| T | transfiguration | The hero is given a new appearance. |
| U | punishment | The villain or false hero is punished. |
| W | wedding | The hero marries or is rewarded with rank, wealth or a throne. |
