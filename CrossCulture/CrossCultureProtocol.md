# Propp's devices across cultures: protocol (FROZEN 2026-10-28 AT THE OWNER'S WORD, BEFORE ANY NATIVE AMERICAN OR EAST ASIAN TALE WAS READ)

**Drafted 2026-10-28 at the owner's word, for `ProppQueue.md` items (l) and (e).** These hypotheses must be frozen before any Native American or East Asian tale is read, and none has been. Nothing here is built. The figures quoted in section 1 are the exploratory first look on the corpora we already have.

## 1. The hypotheses and the metric

**The owner's hypothesis (l):** the Native American and East Asian tales will show many of Propp's devices put to different cultural purposes.

**The owner's metric (l):** what share of all events the catalog covers. It is counted at three lengths:
- single functions (1-grams);
- the pairs (2-grams);
- the longer groups (n-grams).

**The owner's prediction (l):** coverage very low for the Native American tales, better for the East Asian.

**The owner's hypothesis (e):** East Asian tales stress harmony and collaboration. Their troubles close more by reconciliation and reward than by punishment, and the hero's acts help others more.

**Already seen, on the corpora we have** (first look, 2026-10-21, transcribers A and B):

| | Wondertales | Animal tales | Fables | Myths | Tragedies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single functions, the share not X | 68%, 78% | 62%, 57% | 58%, 57% | 59%, 70% | 45%, 48% |
| Of those, the share with the pair partner in the same move | 49% | 54%, 52% | 37%, 33% | 36%, 35% | 31%, 29% |

**The keeper's note:**
- The animal tales, short like Dundes's, are built more of pairs than any other corpus.
- Dundes found little of Propp's long sequence but re-cut his pairs. So the owner's prediction may hold for single functions and groups and fail for pairs.
- The readers are language models whose shared understanding is largely modern and Western. That is a risk to every cross-cultural reading here, stated now and reported with every result.

## 2. The corpora (decision 1)

These are the same instruments, unchanged, two blind transcribers each, A on Opus and B on Sonnet. Public domain only, the status of each source checked and recorded before use. Fifty tales each, as for the animal tales.

Giles's *Strange Stories from a Chinese Studio* (1880) is literary, a recommended exclusion. The owner chooses. The rule recommended: the first 50 tales of the collections chosen, in their printed order.

## 8. Amendment at the owner's word, after the freeze and before any new tale is read

**2026-10-28, X1 and the rule of prediction 6.**

**The reason:** X1 as frozen required the first look of 2026-10-21 to be reproduced, and that first look kept no code.
- Its single-function shares are reproduced to within a point.
- Its pair shares are not: off by up to 7 points, and by up to 4 when counted in order within the move.
- Its definition cannot be recovered from its figures, and fitting one to them would make X1 no test.

**The definitions, written out:**
- **A function row** is a row of `tables/functions.tsv` for the story and transcriber, every reading kept, as the first look counted. Its function is the base symbol of the raw entry (`tragedyScore.base`).
- **Single-function coverage:** the share of function rows whose function is not X.
- **Pairs:** a row is paired when its function is the first of one of Propp's pairs and the second follows it later in the same move, or is the second and the first precedes it in the same move. The share is counted among the rows that are not X. Propp's pairs are the list in `Ngrams/ngrams.py`: γ–δ, ε–ζ, η–θ, D–E, E–F, H–I, Pr–Rs, M–N, A–K, a–K, Q–Ex, Ex–U.
- **Groups:** the share of functions inside one of Propp's blocks, in the blocks reading, by `blockStream.py`'s transform and coverage as `Blocks/parity.py` applies them. Test X2 holds this to the published counts.
- **Punishment (prediction 5):** a story counts when any of its function rows is U.
- **The hero helping another (prediction 6):** the significance layer gives an act's value for the hero only, so it cannot say whether the hero helps another. The rule is from the acts layer instead. Among the acts whose performer is the judged hero, it is the share that are of the kind give, guide or repair and whose undergoer is a character other than the hero.

X1 now requires the program to reproduce `CrossCulture/Reference.txt`, written by these definitions on the existing corpora and committed before any new tale is read, with its single-function shares within a point of the first look's. X2 and X3 are unchanged.

## 9. A known departure from Propp on the card, kept (2026-10-28, at the owner's word)

**The departure.** Card C defines the violation (δ) as "the prohibition is broken, or the order is not obeyed". Propp (p. 27) makes the forms of violation correspond to the forms of interdiction. Since his inverted interdiction (γ²) is an order, his inverted violation is the order *carried out*.

So under the card, an obeyed order is a γ with no δ, and a disobeyed order is a δ. The departure was found in P16 (`Data/KeeperLog.md`, 2026-10-28). It is on every card in use, including the new corpora's `TranscribeRules-C.md`.

**Kept, at the owner's word.** The card is not changed for the new corpora, so that all seven corpora are measured by one instrument, as section 2 requires.

**What follows from it:**
- How a transcriber reads the card's broad γ, every order or only a prohibition, can move a story's score under the story grammar's constraint "γ then δ within the move".
- That difference between the transcribers is to be watched in the new corpora's transcriptions.
- The mend belongs to a later version of the story grammar (`ProppQueue.md` item (p)), not to the card.
