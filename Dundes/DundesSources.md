# The sources of Dundes's North American tales

**Compiled 2026-10-20 from Alan Dundes, *The Morphology of North American Indian Folktales* (doctoral dissertation, Indiana University, 1962), read under fair use for its citations only.** Nothing of its text is reproduced beyond what identifying a source needs. The dissertation is the owner's purchased copy (`Propp PDF/`, held outside the published set).

## How Dundes chose his tales

He studied no single tribe and drew no fixed sample. He chose an illustrative set of tale types, favoring the most widespread so as not to pick only tales that fit, and showed that a tale is not a one-off by its number in Remedios Wycoco (Moore)'s unpublished dissertation, *The Types of North-American Indian Tales* (Indiana University, 1951), which admits only types reported independently at least twice.
**The tale types he names most:** the Star Husband, Orpheus, the Eye-Juggler, Lodge-Boy and Thrown-Away, the Earth-Diver, the Bungling Host, the Dog Husband, and trickster cycles (Coyote, Manabozho, Raven).
**Wycoco numbers cited inline** (31): 251, 252, 253, 261, 262, 302, 304, 305, 307, 353, 532, 561, 801, 901, 971, 1006, 1051, 1101, 1103, 1108, 1166, 1167, 1175, 1176, 1199, 1210, 1222, 1251, 1270, 1351, 1371. Wycoco's dissertation would give, for each type, its versions across the collections; it is the best single index to Dundes's material, if it can be obtained.

## For the project

**Most of the tales are reachable:** the government series and nearly everything published in 1930 or earlier are in the public domain, and many are digitized (the Bureau's reports, the journal's early volumes, the museum papers).
**The single source he cites most, Adamson's Coast Salish tales (1934), is later and must be checked.**
**The collectors are those of early American anthropology** (Boas and his students, the Bureau, the museums), recording texts often with interpreters and sometimes in the original language; their hand on the texts varies, and each collection's own account of its method should be read before its tales are used, as the owner asked.
