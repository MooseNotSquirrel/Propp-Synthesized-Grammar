# A catalog of acts and roles: draft test design

**Draft, 2026-10-12, for the owner's review. Not frozen: nothing below binds until the owner approves it and it is committed as frozen.**

## The question

Propp's functions bind an act to a role and a place: trickery is the villain deceiving a victim before the harm.
The X study found that most recurring acts in the events his functions leave empty are the same acts with other roles or places, and that, given a role-free name for an act, transcribers often prefer it to Propp's function.
This test asks whether a two-layer catalog describes stories better than Propp's functions alone:
the **act layer** says what is done, whoever does it; the **role layer** says who does it to whom, relative to the hero.
Each of Propp's functions becomes an act with its usual roles and place, so his analysis can be recovered from the two layers.

## The instrument

**The acts:** about thirty-five role-free acts, each defined in one line, drawn from the acts inside Propp's functions and the X study's kinds: leave, return, forbid, disobey, seek information, reveal information, deceive, be taken in, harm (strike, seize, steal, expel, kill), want, plead, threaten, send or summon, decide to act, test, respond to a test, give, receive, guide or carry, fight, win, lose, mark, repair the harm, pursue, rescue or escape, arrive unknown, claim falsely, set a task, do the task, recognize, expose or accuse, transform, punish, marry, other.
**The roles:** for the performer and the undergoer, one of: hero, villain, victim, donor, helper, dispatcher, sought-for person or her father, false hero, other.
**The mapping:** a frozen table that turns an act, its two roles and its place (before or after the move's trouble) into Propp's function, where one applies: trickery is deceive, villain to victim, before the trouble; contest and victory by cunning is deceive, hero to villain; and so on.

**The transcription:** fresh transcribers, A on Opus and B on Sonnet, write for each event its act or acts, with the performer's and undergoer's roles, and no Propp function.
**The material:** a sample of held-out stories already transcribed under Propp's functions, so the two readings can be compared event by event; proposed: the 23 Russian test tales, 50 of the held-out fables, and 8 tragedies.

## The measures and the verdict (to be fixed before freezing)

1. **Coverage:** the share of events given an act, against the share given one of Propp's functions in the original transcriptions of the same events. Proposed: at least 10 points higher.
2. **Agreement on acts:** between A and B, events where both write the act over events where either does, against the same figure for Propp's functions. Proposed: at least as high.
3. **Agreement on roles:** reported.
4. **Recovering Propp:** the functions the mapping derives from the two layers, against the functions the original transcribers wrote directly. Proposed: the derived functions agree with the originals at least as well as the two original transcribers agree with each other.

The catalog of acts and roles is **adopted as the instrument** for later work if 1, 2 and 4 hold.

## Cost

One transcription run of the sample, two transcribers: about the size of one of the X study's test stages for the tales and fables, plus one for the tragedies.

## Decisions for the owner

- The list of acts and of roles, before it is frozen.
- The sample size.
- Whether the role layer should name roles relative to the hero, as Propp does, or plain relations (kin, ruler, stranger, rival), which would let the trickster and the failed hero show without assuming who the hero is.
