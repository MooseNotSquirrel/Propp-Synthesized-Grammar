# Keeper log: spheres of action

**2026-10-20. A first look, exploratory** (`explore.py`; `SpheresExplore.txt`, and `SpheresExplore-strict.txt` with the villain's sphere as villainy and pursuit only), at the owner's question whether the other genres have a different set of characters. Each function is credited to the character who performs it; names matched within a story.
**A correction made in the look itself:** Propp's villain sphere holds struggle, but transcribers credit a struggle to whoever fights, often the hero, which inflates the count of characters holding both the hero's and the villain's sphere; the strict reading leaves struggle out.
**What it shows, the keeper's reading:** all seven spheres occur in every genre; the false hero is rare everywhere (2% to 16% of stories), commonest in the tales. The genres differ in how the spheres combine. In tragedy one character holds both the hero's and the villain's sphere in 66% and 72% of plays (strict reading): the tragic hero commits the villainy. In tales 18% and 29%, in myths 27% and 28%, in fables 3% and 4%. The fable fills the villain's sphere far more often than the hero's (44% to 54% against 23% to 25%): it follows the villain. A hero exposed or punished: 22% of tragedies for both transcribers, 7% to 22% elsewhere. The donor who is also the helper, Propp's own example of one character in two spheres, is the commonest combination in the tales. A third to two fifths of characters act only outside the spheres, which include no preparatory function; not yet read further.
