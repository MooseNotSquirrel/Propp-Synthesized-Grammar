# Coding rules: single events

You will code events from tales, one line at a time. Each line stands alone. Work only from these rules and your lines. Do not open other files or search the web. Where a rule leaves you unsure, mark the line `?`; do not invent a rule.

**Code each line by itself.** The lines come from many tales, in no order. Do not try to work out which lines belong together, or what came before or after.

**Each act in an event gets one entry: a symbol from the card, or X.** A symbol stands for an act of a character, defined from the point of view of its significance for the course of the action. Most events narrate one act and get one entry. Where a line narrates two or more distinct acts, give each act that fits a symbol its own entry, in the order the line gives them. A line none of whose acts fits a symbol gets one X.

**Assign the symbol whoever performs it.** The acts on the card are stable elements, independent of how and by whom they are fulfilled. A harmful act by the hero is judged like any other. Read "hero" and "villain" as the roles an act gives, not as fixed characters. Each line names its tale's hero.

**When in doubt between a symbol and X, write X.** A high share of X is a finding, not a failure.

**An act only threatened or intended, and not carried out, is X.**

**A request or demand is the asker's want, not a villainy.** Until something harmful is actually done, a request or demand is the asker's lack (Hab).

**The hero asking another for help is not Poz.** The answer to it is Lom when help is given and Lom− when it is refused.

**A minus marks a negative result:** Lun− for a test the hero fails, Lom− where nothing is given, Gul− where the hero loses the fight, and likewise on any symbol. Use the minus sign − (U+2212).

## The card

| Symbol | What happens |
|---|---|
| Wob | A false hero claims what is not theirs: a deed, a reward, a quality. |
| Lun | The hero responds to that test or request: passes or fails it, helps or refuses, withstands or yields. |
| Nep | The hero is given a new appearance. |
| Zab | The hero decides or agrees to act against the misfortune or lack. |
| Sef | The prohibition is broken, or the order is not obeyed. |
| Rav | The villain tries to find out something about the victim: where they are, or what they have. |
| Bip | The task is accomplished. |
| Lom | The hero gets something or someone that will help: given, earned, bought, found, seized, or a helper offering service. |
| Mur | Someone is forbidden to do something, or ordered to do something. |
| Vek | The misfortune or lack is made known to someone who might act on it; the hero is asked, ordered, allowed, or sent to act. A lament or complaint to no one who might act is X. |
| Mip | A member of a household, or the one who protects it, goes away or dies, leaving others unprotected. |
| Dov | The victim is taken in by the deception and so unwittingly helps the villain. A victim who refuses or sees through the trick has not performed Dov: write that act X. |
| Suv | The hero is led, carried, or shown the way to what is sought. |
| Vig | The hero and the villain meet in direct combat or contest. |
| Fub | The hero returns to where they set out from. |
| Yul | The hero marries or is rewarded with rank, wealth or a throne. |
| Qet | The hero arrives, at home or elsewhere, without being recognized. |
| Tiv | The characters and their situation are introduced. Not an act on this card; write it only for an event that does nothing but introduce. An event that narrates an act is never Tiv. |
| Gop | The hero is pursued. |
| Dek | The hero is saved from pursuit or from destruction. |
| Hab | Someone lacks or desires something: food, safety, a mate, a possession, a quality they do not have. |
| Hux | The hero is recognized. |
| Poz | The hero is tested, questioned, asked for help, or attacked by someone who may then give help. |
| Jem | The original misfortune or lack is set right: the loss restored, the want satisfied, the victim freed. |
| Nar | A difficult task or test is set. |
| Kib | The villain or false hero is punished. |
| Baf | The false hero or the villain is exposed. |
| Sog | The villain receives that information. |
| Gul | The villain is defeated: killed, beaten, outwitted, or driven off. |
| Zin | The hero is marked: wounded, or given a token that can later identify them. |
| Pov | The hero leaves home or sets out. |
| Rud | The villain, often in disguise or with false words, tries to deceive the victim in order to get hold of them or what they have. |
| Dox | The villain does harm: kills, carries off, steals, injures, drives out, deceives to ruin, or otherwise causes loss or misfortune. |
| Kem | A misfortune that forces the victim to accept the villain's deceitful bargain. |

## Format

Write one line for each of your lines, in the order given:

```
e0000 | <entries, separated by spaces, or X> | <? if unsure, or -> | <a note of at most six words, or ->
```

Write nothing else in the file. Your report: only the identifiers of any lines you could not code, then the two closing lines your prompt asks for.
