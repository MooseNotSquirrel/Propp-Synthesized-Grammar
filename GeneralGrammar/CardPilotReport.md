## The limit that matters most: the masking failed

**Both S readers named Propp in their closing line, unprompted by anything on the card.** Reader A named "Vladimir Propp's scheme of functions"; B named his thirty-one functions and *Morphology of the Folktale*. They recognized the definitions' content. The protocol said a reader might, and it does.

So the pilot answers a narrower question than the one that prompted it:
- **Answered, for the wondertales:** the card's listed order and his symbols do not write the order in. Taking them away changes it little.
- **Not answered:** whether readers who know his scheme write his order in from memory. Part 1 cannot speak to that, as the S readers knew it too.

## Beside the reading

- **Run noise.** The old strings of these tales give 0.828 and 0.751; new H gives 0.712 and 0.734. Same card, a different run, a difference of up to 0.12, as large as the rule's margin of 0.10.
- **Coverage rose under S:** given a function, A 65% to 72%, B 69% to 87%. B's strings grew from 23.5 to 31.1 units a story. The neutral card drew more functions and fewer X. Why is not known.
- **The preparatory units stay mid-move in both cells:** 83% of them after the first A or a in H, 85% in S. The move rule was the same in both, as designed, so this does not test it.
- **Pairs.** His pairs adjacent in at least 2 stories: A, 9 under H and 7 under S; B, 10 and 7. M-N and A-K dropped out under S for both. The numbers are small.
- **The tool,** on 15 stories, below its tested 20: bigram in all four; order tau 0.84, 0.86 for A, 0.61, 0.56 for B.

## Cost

The stages ran with no failures. The owner's usage figures would give the first measure of cost; the keeper cannot see them.

## What follows

**The keeper's recommendation, not adopted: hold part 2.** It would repeat the same design, and the East Asian readers would likely recognize the card as well. A better test is one memory cannot help: code each event **shuffled, apart from its tale,** so a reader who knows the scheme cannot use a tale's place in it. If his order still appears when the labels are put back in story order, it comes from the events and not the reader's expectation. It needs a protocol of its own, frozen by the owner.
